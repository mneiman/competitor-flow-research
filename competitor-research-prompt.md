Provisional candidates, to verify rather than assume: CDK, Reynolds ERA-IGNITE, Dealertrack DMS, Tekion, PBS Systems, Autosoft, Auto/Mate (core DMS); Xtime, Dealer-FX and myKaarma (specialist service platforms). FOR EACH PRODUCT: Record current name/owner, category, target dealerships, evidence of adoption, known strengths, dated user-review praise and recurring complaints including sample size and reviewer role. Prioritize public official manuals, help articles, product demos/videos and screenshots. Capture source URL, date/version, relevant screenshot source or video timestamp, actor, screen name, fields, actions, status changes, handoffs and documented business rules. Distinguish observed/documented behavior, vendor claims, user reports, inference, unknowns, and proposed improvements. Public UI behavior does not establish private backend architecture. Do not invent screens, APIs, integrations or capabilities. Where screens are inaccessible, preserve the gap and use permitted public sources. COMMON SCENARIOS: Appointment and vehicle/VIN/mileage identification; opening a repair order from customer concerns and service menus; job-level estimates and versions; customer authorization; technician dispatch, inspection, evidence, labor estimates versus actual and billed hours; part lookup by number or guided manufacturer/brand/category search with fitment validation; distinguish manufacturer from purchasing supplier; common inventory for counter sales and RO demand; requesting, reserving, purchasing/backorders, receiving, issuing, returning and replenishing parts; prepaid dealer/OEM maintenance with remaining benefits and redemption; factory warranty, service contracts, deductibles, recalls, goodwill, dealer/internal pay and split payers; agreed discounts and permissions; completion versus ready-for-pickup; invoices, claims and reconciliation. Coverage eligibility, customer authorization, claim approval and payment are different states. Do not assume a plan covers every job or that warranty equals a zero-price repair.

## Later refinement: maps and data organization

Make an overview and at least two concrete drill-downs: Counter sale, and Request/fulfill parts for a repair-order job. Example branch: How find the part? -> Which categories/brands/identifiers? -> Who owns the catalog? -> Are part numbers unique within brand? -> Supersessions and vehicle fitment? -> Which legacy ID maps to the new identity? -> Who may update this data during coexistence? Another: How much needed? -> Job template versus technician input? -> each/set/litre and supplier pack conversion? -> quantity already reserved and inbound? -> shortage? -> transfer or supplier order? -> who approves, receives and fulfills? -> partial delivery, cancellation or return? Each answer should branch to deeper business, data, UI and integration questions only where relevant, rather than repeat a form.

Show candidate logical units (catalog, inventory availability/reservations/movements, demand from counter sales/RO jobs, purchasing, receiving, fulfillment, pricing/billing) and dependencies without asserting separate services or separate databases. Link questions to proposed screens and conceptual entities/relationships. Marina asked how many tables are needed: derive candidate entities from rules and ownership; do not invent an authoritative table count before inspecting legacy schemas. Include a provisional conceptual data model with explicit unknowns, and coexistence questions about source of truth, legacy/new ID mapping, single-writer ownership, synchronization/outbox where appropriate, reconciliation, retries and cutover. Do not assume the legacy schemas, private code, or labels in the reference image have been verified.
